

<h1>Privacy Policy</h1>
<h2>ECG Quiz: EKG Rhythms & ABG</h2>

<p class="summary">
  <strong>ECG Quiz has no account and asks you for nothing.</strong> It shows
  ads, and it sends Google anonymous usage counts and a crash report if it
  crashes. Your practice - every answer, score and streak - stays on your
  phone and is never uploaded. No permission dialog ever appears, and the
  quiz itself works with no internet connection.
</p>

<h2>1. What we never collect</h2>
<p>We do not collect, store, sell or share any personal information.
Specifically, the app never collects:</p>
<ul>
  <li>your name, email address or any account details - there is no sign-in;</li>
  <li>your location;</li>
  <li>contacts, photos, camera, microphone or files;</li>
  <li>health data of any kind, yours or anyone else's;</li>
  <li>anything you type;</li>
  <li>your answers, scores, streaks or study history - these stay on your
      phone, and section 2 says what happens to them.</li>
</ul>

<h2>1a. What is sent to Google</h2>
<p>The app uses two Google Firebase services. Neither one can identify you,
and neither is given anything about how you are doing in the app.</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Firebase Analytics</strong>, for counts: how many people open
      the app, which practice modes get used, how many rounds are finished.
      Google's SDK attaches a randomly generated app instance ID, your device
      model and Android version, your country, and your device's advertising
      ID. It is not linked to a name or an account, because the app has
      neither.</li>
  <li><strong>Firebase Crashlytics</strong>, so that a crash on a phone we
      have never seen can be fixed. If the app crashes it sends the stack
      trace, the device model and Android version, and a random installation
      identifier. Nothing you typed or answered is part of a crash report.</li>
</ul>
<p>Both are operated by Google and handled under
<a href="https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy">Firebase's privacy
terms</a> and Google's privacy policy.</p>

<h2>1b. Advertising</h2>
<p>The app shows ads supplied by <strong>Google AdMob</strong>: a banner on
the home, library, progress and badges screens, and an occasional full-screen
ad after you finish a round of practice. There are no ads while you are
answering a question or reading a strip.</p>
<p>To choose which ads to show, AdMob receives your device's advertising ID
along with technical information such as your device model, Android version,
language and approximate region. It is never told your name, your email, or
anything about your practice - not which rhythms you study, not your scores,
not your streak. AdMob's use of this information is covered by
<a href="https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6128543">Google's advertising
policies</a> and Google's privacy policy.</p>
<p>You are in control of the advertising ID: Android Settings lets you reset
it, or delete it entirely so that ads are no longer personalised. Doing either
does not stop the app working.</p>

<h2>2. What the app stores on your device</h2>
<p>So that your practice carries over between sessions, the app saves the
following in Android's private storage for this app:</p>
<ul>
  <li>how many questions you have answered and how many were right;</li>
  <li>your current and best streak, and which days you practised;</li>
  <li>your accuracy for each rhythm and each blood gas disorder;</li>
  <li>badges earned, mock-exam scores, and your settings.</li>
</ul>
<p>This information never leaves your device. It is not readable by us or by
any other app. It is removed when you clear the app's data or uninstall the
app.</p>

<h2>3. Internet access</h2>
<p>The app works completely offline. Every rhythm strip, every blood gas and
every explanation is generated on your phone, so the whole app - including all
of your practice - runs in airplane mode with nothing missing.</p>
<p>The network is used only by the services in sections 1a and 1b. With no
connection they simply do not report and no ads are fetched; the quiz itself
behaves exactly the same.</p>

<h2>4. Permissions</h2>
<p><strong>No permission dialog ever appears.</strong> Every permission the app
declares is granted at install time by Android; none of them is the kind you
are asked to approve, and none gives the app access to your camera,
microphone, location, contacts, photos or files.</p>
<p>For completeness, the released app declares:
<code>INTERNET</code>, <code>ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE</code> and
<code>WAKE_LOCK</code>, which Firebase needs in order to report;
<code>AD_ID</code>, <code>ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID</code> and
<code>ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION</code>, which Firebase Analytics uses to
read the device's advertising ID;
<code>BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE</code>, which lets Google Play tell
Analytics how the app was found; and
<code>com.ecgquiz.app.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION</code>, which
Google's AndroidX libraries add to every app targeting Android 13 or later
and which the app grants only to itself.</p>

<h2>5. Third parties</h2>
<p>Google only: Firebase for the two services in section 1a, and AdMob for the
ads in section 1b. There are no social media SDKs and no attribution services
beyond Analytics itself. Nothing about your practice or your results is sent
to anyone, including Google.</p>

<h2>6. When the app opens another app</h2>
<p>Two buttons hand off to an app you choose:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Report a question</strong> opens your email app with a draft
      already written. The draft contains only the name of the rhythm and the
      numeric seed the strip was generated from - enough for us to reproduce
      the exact question, and nothing about you. Nothing is sent unless you
      press send in your own email app.</li>
  <li><strong>Privacy policy</strong> opens this page in your browser.</li>
  <li><strong>Rate on Play</strong>, offered occasionally after a round of
      practice, opens this app's page in the Google Play app or your browser.
      Nothing about you is attached to that link, and the app is not told
      whether you left a review. Declining changes nothing.</li>
</ul>
<p>Once you are in your email app or browser, that app's own privacy policy
applies to what happens there.</p>

<h2>7. Children</h2>
<p>ECG Quiz is a study tool for nursing, paramedic and medical students and
for clinicians. It is not directed at children, and it is not designed or
marketed for them. It asks nobody for personal information at any age.</p>

<h2>8. Your rights</h2>
<p>Data-protection rights such as access, correction, deletion and portability
exist to give you control over information a company holds about you. We hold
nothing that identifies you: your practice is on your phone, and what Firebase
reports carries no name or account.</p>
<p>In practice this means: your progress is deleted by clearing the app's data
or uninstalling the app; the advertising ID that Analytics reads can be reset
or deleted from your device's Android settings at any time; and questions
about anything Google holds can be raised with us at the address below.</p>

<h2>9. Not a medical device</h2>
<p>ECG Quiz is an educational study tool. It is not a medical device and does
not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any medical condition. All rhythm strips
and blood gas values are computer-generated teaching examples built from
published criteria; they are not recordings or records of any real person. The
app does not read, interpret or store patient data. Always consult a qualified
healthcare professional for medical advice.</p>

<h2>10. Changes to this policy</h2>
<p>If this policy changes, the revised version will be posted on this page with
a new "last updated" date at the top. If a future version of the app ever
collects something this page does not describe, this page will say so before
that version ships.</p>
